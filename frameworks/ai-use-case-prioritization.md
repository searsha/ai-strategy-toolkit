# AI Use Case Prioritization Framework

A practical framework for evaluating and comparing potential AI initiatives before investing significant time, funding, or organizational resources.

The goal is to help organizations distinguish between AI opportunities that are strategically valuable and realistically implementable versus those that may be interesting but premature, overly complex, or unnecessarily risky.

## How to Use This Framework

Evaluate each proposed AI use case across seven dimensions.

Score each dimension from **1 to 5**, where:

* **1 = Very Low**
* **2 = Low**
* **3 = Moderate**
* **4 = High**
* **5 = Very High**

The framework can be used during initial discovery, portfolio planning, innovation reviews, or AI governance discussions.

---

## 1. Strategic Alignment

How directly does the use case support organizational priorities?

Consider:

* Alignment with organizational strategy
* Connection to business or mission objectives
* Relevance to current priorities
* Executive or stakeholder sponsorship
* Potential contribution to competitive advantage

**Score: 1–5**

---

## 2. Business Value & Impact

What meaningful value could the AI initiative create?

Consider:

* Revenue growth
* Cost reduction
* Productivity improvement
* Improved decision-making
* Customer or stakeholder experience
* Employee experience
* Quality improvement
* Scalability

**Score: 1–5**

---

## 3. Implementation Feasibility

How realistic is implementation given the organization's current capabilities?

Consider:

* Technical complexity
* Required integrations
* Availability of appropriate AI solutions
* Infrastructure requirements
* Implementation timeline
* Internal expertise
* Vendor dependency

**Score: 1–5**

A higher score indicates **greater feasibility**.

---

## 4. Data Readiness

Does the organization have the data needed to support the use case?

Consider:

* Data availability
* Data quality
* Accessibility
* Data ownership
* Privacy considerations
* Security requirements
* Integration across systems

**Score: 1–5**

---

## 5. Organizational & Adoption Readiness

How prepared are the people and processes affected by the initiative?

Consider:

* Leadership support
* Employee readiness
* Workflow maturity
* Change-management requirements
* Training needs
* Trust in AI-enabled processes
* Clarity of ownership

**Score: 1–5**

---

## 6. Responsible AI & Risk

What level of risk does the use case introduce?

Consider:

* Privacy
* Security
* Bias and fairness
* Regulatory or legal exposure
* Explainability
* Reputational risk
* Impact of incorrect outputs
* Sensitivity of decisions being influenced

For this category:

* **5 = Low risk / easily manageable**
* **1 = High risk / significant controls required**

**Score: 1–5**

---

## 7. Human Oversight & Accountability

Can clear human responsibility remain within the AI-enabled process?

Consider:

* Whether humans can review important outputs
* Whether decisions can be challenged or corrected
* Whether escalation processes exist
* Whether responsibility remains clearly assigned
* Whether the AI system supports rather than obscures accountability

**Score: 1–5**

---

# Scoring

Add the seven category scores together.

**Maximum score: 35**

### 29–35: Prioritize

Strong candidate for implementation or a structured pilot.

The use case demonstrates high strategic value, organizational readiness, and manageable risk.

### 23–28: Pilot

Promising opportunity that should be tested through a defined pilot before broader implementation.

Specific gaps should be addressed during the pilot.

### 17–22: Explore

Potential value exists, but significant questions remain around readiness, feasibility, data, adoption, or risk.

Further discovery is recommended.

### 7–16: Defer

The use case is currently unlikely to justify implementation.

Reassess if organizational priorities, technology, data readiness, or operating conditions change.

---

# Portfolio View

Organizations should avoid evaluating AI opportunities in isolation.

Once multiple use cases have been scored, compare them across the portfolio to identify:

* High-value, low-complexity opportunities
* Strategic investments requiring longer-term capability building
* Quick-win pilots
* High-risk initiatives requiring additional governance
* Initiatives that should be deferred

The objective is not to implement AI everywhere, rather to identify where AI can create meaningful value while remaining feasible, responsible, and aligned with organizational strategy.
