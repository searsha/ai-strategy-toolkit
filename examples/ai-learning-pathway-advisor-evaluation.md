# Sample AI Use Case Evaluation

## AI Learning Pathway & Course Navigation Advisor

This example demonstrates how the AI Use Case Prioritization Framework can be applied to an AI-enabled learning and workforce development initiative.


## Use Case Overview

**Use Case Name:**
AI Learning Pathway & Course Navigation Advisor

**Business Area / Function:**
Learning & Development / Higher Education / Workforce Development

### Problem or Opportunity

Learners are increasingly presented with a large and growing ecosystem of courses, credentials, short-form learning opportunities, degree pathways, and professional development resources.

While greater choice creates opportunity, it can also create friction.

Learners may struggle to determine:

* Which learning opportunities are most relevant to their goals
* Where they should begin
* Which skills they already possess
* Which courses build logically upon one another
* How individual learning experiences connect to broader credentials or career goals
* Whether a particular learning pathway is appropriate for their experience level

Traditional catalogs and search tools place much of the navigation burden on the learner.

### Proposed AI Application

Develop an AI-enabled learning advisor embedded within the digital learning environment.

The advisor would help learners explore available educational opportunities by considering factors such as:

* Professional goals
* Current skills
* Prior learning
* Areas of interest
* Desired competencies
* Available time
* Preferred learning format
* Career aspirations

The system could recommend relevant learning opportunities, explain why they may be useful, identify potential learning sequences, and direct learners toward appropriate resources.

The AI would support navigation and discovery rather than independently making admissions, academic progression, or high-impact educational decisions.

### Primary Stakeholders

* Learners
* Faculty
* Academic and learning-program leaders
* Student support teams
* Workforce development teams
* Learning experience designers
* Technology teams
* Data and privacy stakeholders
* AI governance stakeholders


# Evaluation

## 1. Strategic Alignment

**Score: 5 / 5**

An AI-enabled learning advisor strongly aligns with organizational strategies focused on:

* Personalized learning
* Learner experience
* Digital transformation
* Workforce development
* Lifelong learning
* Credential innovation
* Increased accessibility to educational pathways
* Scalable learner support

It could also create an infrastructure layer connecting individual courses, short-form learning opportunities, credentials, and larger educational pathways.


## 2. Business Value & Impact

**Score: 5 / 5**

Potential value extends beyond simple course recommendations.

The system could improve:

* Learner discovery
* Engagement
* Course and credential utilization
* Learner persistence
* Navigation across complex learning portfolios
* Awareness of relevant educational opportunities
* Workforce skill development
* Cross-program pathway visibility

The platform may also generate valuable insights into emerging learner interests, skill demands, and gaps within an organization's learning portfolio.


## 3. Implementation Feasibility

**Score: 4 / 5**

The core generative AI capabilities required to create a conversational learning advisor are already available.

The more significant implementation challenge involves creating reliable access to structured information regarding:

* Learning outcomes
* Course content
* Prerequisites
* Credential relationships
* Skill mappings
* Program requirements
* Learner eligibility

Successful implementation would likely require integration with learning-management systems, course catalogs, credential systems, or other institutional data sources.

A limited pilot using a defined portfolio of learning opportunities would reduce implementation complexity.


## 4. Data Readiness

**Score: 3 / 5**

Data readiness represents one of the largest potential constraints.

Course and program information may exist across multiple systems and may not always be structured consistently.

Potential data requirements include:

* Course descriptions
* Learning objectives
* Skills and competencies
* Prerequisites
* Credential requirements
* Program relationships
* Learner-provided goals and preferences

Organizations would need to determine whether personal learner information should be incorporated and, if so, establish appropriate privacy and access controls.

Improving metadata and learning-content taxonomy may be necessary before the system can provide highly reliable recommendations.


## 5. Organizational & Adoption Readiness

**Score: 4 / 5**

Learners are increasingly familiar with conversational AI interfaces, which could reduce adoption barriers.

However, successful implementation would require involvement from:

* Academic leadership
* Faculty
* Advising teams
* Learning designers
* Technology teams
* Student or learner support functions

The system's role would need to be clearly communicated.

It should complement rather than replace professional academic advising where human judgment, policy interpretation, or individual circumstances require additional support.


## 6. Responsible AI & Risk

**Score: 3 / 5**

The use case introduces moderate risk because inaccurate recommendations could affect a learner's educational decisions.

Primary considerations include:

* Hallucinated course or program information
* Outdated requirements
* Inappropriate recommendations
* Bias within recommendations
* Privacy of learner information
* Accessibility
* Transparency regarding why recommendations were generated
* Overreliance on AI guidance

The system should retrieve information from approved and current institutional sources rather than rely solely on general model knowledge.

High-impact decisions should remain outside the authority of the AI system.


## 7. Human Oversight & Accountability

**Score: 5 / 5**

Clear boundaries can preserve appropriate human oversight.

The AI advisor may:

* Explain educational options
* Recommend learning resources
* Help learners compare pathways
* Surface relevant opportunities
* Ask questions to clarify learner goals

The AI advisor should not independently:

* Approve admission
* Determine academic eligibility
* Override prerequisite requirements
* Make progression decisions
* Issue credentials
* Provide authoritative interpretations of academic policy

Learners should be directed to an appropriate human advisor when questions involve policy, exceptions, eligibility, or high-impact educational decisions.

## Recommendation

**Recommended Classification: PRIORITIZE**

With a score of **29/35**, the AI Learning Pathway & Course Navigation Advisor represents a strong candidate for a structured pilot.

The opportunity demonstrates significant strategic value and strong alignment with emerging models of personalized, lifelong, and workforce-focused learning.

The greatest challenges are not necessarily the AI capabilities themselves.

They are likely to involve **data architecture, learning-content metadata, governance, integration, and clearly defining the boundaries between AI guidance and human advising.**


## Recommended Pilot

Begin with a limited learning portfolio rather than attempting institution-wide implementation.

For example, the pilot could include:

* A defined collection of short-form courses
* Several related credentials
* Clearly mapped skills and learning outcomes
* A controlled learner population

The AI advisor could help users answer questions such as:

* *Where should I start?*
* *Which learning opportunities align with my career goal?*
* *What should I take after completing this course?*
* *What skills would I develop through this pathway?*
* *How are these credentials connected?*

Pilot results could then determine whether the system should expand into additional programs or learning environments.


# Implementation Considerations

Before implementation, the organization should:

1. Establish an authoritative source of course and credential information.
2. Develop consistent metadata for learning outcomes, skills, and prerequisites.
3. Define what learner information the AI may access.
4. Establish privacy and data-governance requirements.
5. Define clear boundaries between AI navigation and human advising.
6. Create escalation pathways to human support.
7. Test recommendations for accuracy, bias, and consistency.
8. Establish processes for updating information when courses or requirements change.
9. Capture learner feedback.
10. Monitor recommendations over time.


# Success Measures

Potential measures include:

### Learner Experience

* Learner satisfaction
* Recommendation usefulness
* Time required to identify relevant learning
* Engagement with recommended content

### Organizational Impact

* Course discovery
* Enrollment conversion
* Credential progression
* Utilization of learning portfolios
* Reduced navigation friction
* Reduced routine advising inquiries

### AI Performance

* Recommendation accuracy
* Rate of unsupported or incorrect recommendations
* Successful human escalation
* User correction frequency
* Consistency across similar learner scenarios


# Strategic Consideration

The long-term opportunity extends beyond building a chatbot.

A well-designed AI learning advisor could become an **intelligence layer across an organization's learning ecosystem**, connecting learners with educational opportunities based on skills, goals, experiences, and changing workforce needs.

The strategic question therefore becomes not simply:

**"Can AI recommend a course?"**

but:

**"How can AI help individuals navigate increasingly complex learning ecosystems while preserving trust, human agency, and responsible decision-making?"**
