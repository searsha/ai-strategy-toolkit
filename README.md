## So, what's this?

There is a lot of conversation about **what AI can do**.

I'm more interested in what comes next.

**Should we use it?**
**Where does it create real value?**
**Are people actually ready for it?**
**What could go wrong?**
**Where should humans stay involved?**
**And how will we know whether any of this actually worked?**

That's what this toolkit is about.

The **AI Strategy Toolkit** is my evolving collection of frameworks, templates, and applied ideas for navigating the space between:

<div align="center">

### *"We should do something with AI."*

and

### *"We know exactly why we're doing this, how we'll do it responsibly, and what success looks like."*

</div>

<br>

# AI Readiness, without the buzzwords

My readiness framework looks across eight dimensions and produces a score out of 40.

But the number isn't really the interesting part.

The interesting part is discovering **where the gaps are.**

|     Score | Where You Are    | What It Means                                                    |
| --------: | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
|  **8–15** | **Foundational** | There's groundwork to do first.                                  |
| **16–23** | **Emerging**     | Good momentum, but still fragmented.                             |
| **24–31** | **Developing**   | Ready to move, with some gaps to address.                        |
| **32–36** | **AI-Ready**     | Strong foundation for coordinated adoption.                      |
| **37–40** | **Scaling**      | AI capability is becoming part of how the organization operates. |

<br>

An organization with incredible technology and poor workforce readiness isn't truly AI-ready.

Neither is an organization full of excited employees without the data, governance, or infrastructure to support them.

**Readiness is the whole system.**

<br>

# First Applied Idea

## AI Learning Pathway & Course Navigation Advisor

This one is especially interesting to me.

As learning ecosystems grow, people are increasingly expected to navigate a massive mix of:

**Courses + short-form learning + credentials + skills + degrees + professional development + career pathways**

And we're still largely asking them to figure out the connections themselves.

The idea:

> What if AI could help someone understand not just **what course exists**, but **what makes sense for them next?**

An AI-enabled learning advisor could connect:

<div align="center">

### GOALS → SKILLS → LEARNING → CREDENTIALS → OPPORTUNITY

</div>

But that creates much more interesting questions than simply building a chatbot.

What information should it know?

How should recommendations be generated?

What happens if it's wrong?

Where does AI navigation end and human advising begin?

How do we avoid creating a system that confidently sends someone down the wrong path?

How do we measure whether personalization is actually helping?

That's the kind of AI problem I enjoy thinking about.

### Evaluation Result

**29 / 35 — PRIORITIZE**

Strong opportunity.

But the biggest implementation questions aren't necessarily about the model.

They're about:

**data architecture • learning metadata • governance • trust • human boundaries • organizational readiness**

# Things I believe about AI strategy

### Strategy before technology.

The problem comes first. The tool comes later.

---

### Human-centered doesn't mean anti-automation.

Sometimes automation is absolutely the right answer.

The question is whether we're removing unnecessary work or removing judgment that actually matters.

---

### Governance shouldn't kill innovation.

Good governance should help people understand **where they can move quickly, where they need guardrails, and where additional scrutiny is warranted.**

---

### AI literacy isn't one skill.

Executives, managers, technical teams, educators, frontline employees, and strategists don't need identical AI capabilities.

They need the **right capabilities for the decisions and work they're responsible for.**

---

### Adoption is part of the strategy.

If the technology works beautifully and nobody trusts it, understands it, or wants to use it...

it doesn't work beautifully.

---

### "We launched it" is not an outcome.

AI success should show up somewhere meaningful:

**time • quality • experience • growth • learning • capacity • access • decision-making • value**

Otherwise, we're measuring activity instead of impact.

# Why I'm building this

I'm fascinated by the messy middle between **AI possibility and organizational reality**.

The technology is moving incredibly fast.

Organizations, policies, processes, workforce capabilities, learning systems, and cultures don't always move at the same speed.

That's where strategy matters.

My interests sit at the intersection of:

<div align="center">

**AI Strategy**   ·  
**Responsible AI**   ·  
**Business Transformation**

**Workforce Innovation**   ·  
**Learning Innovation**   ·

I'm especially interested in the question:
